# Takahe Air Company History

## 2019 — Founding

Takahe Air Holdings Limited was incorporated on 14 March 2019. Hana Reweti, previously General Manager of Regional Operations at a larger New Zealand carrier, and Tomas Vella, an aviation systems engineer, raised NZD 6.5 million in seed funding from Tūrangawaewae Capital and three regional council investment arms.

The first route, Wellington to Westport, launched on 2 September 2019 with a single leased ATR 72-600. It sold out for the first eleven days of operation.

## 2020 — The pause

Border closures and domestic restrictions grounded the fleet for 94 days. Takahe Air kept all 38 staff employed through the wage subsidy scheme and used the downtime to build its own booking platform rather than licence one. Tomas Vella has since described this as "the most expensive decision we ever got right".

The airline returned to service on 18 June 2020 with two aircraft and four routes.

## 2021 — TakaheCargo

With passenger demand still recovering, Takahe Air launched TakaheCargo in April 2021, converting two aircraft to freight configuration overnight and back to passenger configuration for day flights. Freight grew to 31 percent of revenue by the end of that year.

## 2022 — Series A

A NZD 24 million Series A round funded the purchase of four aircraft outright, ending the airline's dependence on leases. The Christchurch operations base opened in November.

## 2023 — Series B and SkyPoints

Tūrangawaewae Capital led a NZD 55 million Series B at a valuation of NZD 310 million. The SkyPoints loyalty programme launched in August 2023 and reached 100,000 members within six months.

## 2024 — TakaheConnect

The booking platform built during the 2020 shutdown was opened to third parties as TakaheConnect, a B2B booking API. Travel agencies and corporate travel managers could now book Takahe Air inventory directly.

## 2025 — Profitability

Takahe Air recorded its first full year of profitability, posting NZD 12.1 million net profit on NZD 189 million revenue.

## 2026 — Fifteen destinations

The Oamaru and Whakatāne routes launched in February 2026, bringing the network to fifteen destinations. FY2026 revenue reached NZD 218 million.
